STEP BY STEP

What happens when you vote?

The voting experience should be simple for students even though several security controls operate behind the scenes.

01
IDENTITY

You authenticate securely

The system verifies the student's authorized university identity before allowing access to the ballot.

  • University account authentication
  • Eligibility verification
  • Optional multi-factor authentication
02
ELIGIBILITY

The system checks your ballot status

ALU-ELECT checks whether you are eligible for the election and whether your voting opportunity has already been used.

  • Eligible voter check
  • Election participation status
  • Prevention of duplicate voting
03
BALLOT

You make your choices privately

The ballot interface presents the available positions and candidates. Students select their preferred candidates before reviewing the ballot.

  • One controlled ballot per eligible voter
  • Clear candidate information
  • Review before submission
04
PRIVACY

Your ballot is separated from your identity

The architecture should separate voter eligibility information from the secret ballot record so that verification does not reveal who a voter selected.

  • Identity and ballot separation
  • Protected ballot data
  • No public display of individual selections
05
CRYPTOGRAPHY

The vote receives cryptographic protection

Cryptographic techniques can be used to protect ballot information and create verifiable commitments to election records.

  • Encryption
  • Hashing
  • Digital signatures where required
06
RECORDING

The ballot is recorded

The protected vote is recorded by the election system and the relevant election state is updated.

  • Transaction validation
  • Secure database storage
  • Election-state updates
07
BLOCKCHAIN

Election integrity is anchored

Important election events or cryptographic commitments can be anchored to a permissioned blockchain or distributed ledger operated by authorized election participants.

  • Tamper-evident records
  • Distributed verification
  • Controlled network membership
08
RECEIPT

You receive a verification reference

After successful submission, the student receives a reference that can later be used to verify that the ballot record exists.

  • Unique verification reference
  • Record status
  • No candidate selection revealed
09
LIVE ELECTION

Results update during the election

Where permitted by the university's election rules, provisional statistics and candidate totals can be displayed through the live results dashboard.

  • Candidate vote totals
  • Percentages and race leads
  • Turnout statistics
10
CERTIFICATION

Final results are audited and certified

Once voting closes, authorized election officials can perform the final reconciliation, review audit records and certify the official results.

  • Final vote reconciliation
  • Audit review
  • Official certification
BLOCKCHAIN & ELECTION INTEGRITY

Why use blockchain?

Blockchain is not being used simply because it sounds advanced. Its purpose is to help create an independently checkable, tamper-evident history of important election records.

ALU-ELECT should use a permissioned blockchain architecture rather than putting private student information or raw ballots onto a public blockchain.

✓

Tamper evidence

Unauthorized changes to anchored records can become detectable.

✓

Shared verification

Authorized participating institutions or election authorities can independently validate ledger information.

✓

Auditability

Important election events can have a stronger, verifiable audit trail.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Blockchain is one security layer, not the entire system.

A trustworthy election requires multiple controls working together.

✓

Blockchain can help with

  • Tamper-evident records
  • Distributed auditability
  • Cryptographic commitments
  • Election-state integrity
!

Blockchain does not automatically solve

  • Weak passwords
  • Compromised devices
  • Poor authentication
  • Incorrect election configuration
  • Social engineering
DEFENCE IN DEPTH

Multiple layers protect the election

ALU-ELECT should not rely on a single security mechanism. Each layer addresses a different type of risk.

01
🔑

Authentication

Strong university identity verification helps prevent unauthorized users from entering the election.

02
🧾

Eligibility

Election rules determine who can vote and which positions a voter can participate in.

03
🔒

Encryption

Sensitive information should be protected while moving through the system and while stored.

04
🗳

Ballot Privacy

Voter identity and secret ballot information should be architecturally separated.

05
⛓

Distributed Ledger

06
👥

Administrative Controls
